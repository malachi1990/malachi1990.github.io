## Musing 201

Yesterday, I was reading the didache, and it really hit home how different 
1st century Christianity was to our times. One of the clearest things is in how 
it clearly assumed that it was normal for there to be *daily* gatherings of believers that
one could meet with. 

While there is much in Acts and the Epistles to suggest this as well, at times those references
feel a bit more like summary in nature, rather than a more in your face "Christians literally are meeting 7 days out of
the week to support one another".

Which hammers home how for the early Christians, the Church was their found family. We in this day
functionally maintain the equality of blood family with the church (and in many cases, rightfully so, since Scripture
itself calls out how those who don't take care of their families are worse than unbelievers), but in the didache one 
can really feel how the believers are the family one should look towards. 

While it isn't inspired, I do think the words of the didache should be pondered and let it confront us about how shallow the
requirements of the modern day church really are. Even recognizing the differing cultural norms at play, may it challenge 
us into following Christ more faithfully.
