## Musing 196

One of the reasons I like true crime is that it confronts us with ugly situations where there is often no clear answer.
And so, if we are to honestly engage with those situations, it requires us to acknowledge that life is often more gray than we like.

Justice for everyone in a situation may mean some level of mercy for the guilty so that innocent parties are not hurt, or 
because we must recognize our own fallibility in determining what actually happened.  Sometimes the guilt is caused by situations
where a person wanted to get out of a situation, but did it the wrong way.

In our era, we see more and more people looking for every excuse to avoid justice through accountability. Sometimes, there is
legitimate reason to lesson accountability for a person. Oftentimes, we must simply remember that judicial mercy is mercy
precisely because it is unearned. 

But alongside the accountability aspect to justice, there is also a protective element and a restorative element. Protective
in that justice which is given should result in a safer society because the sin in it is adequately punished. Restorative because
insofar as it is possible, justice should seek to restore an injured party's loss and then some. 

But the reality is this - we will not have perfect justice in this world. While some cases may get it, in some cases an 
innocent party is convicted of a crime. Sometimes the guilty get away with it. But there comes a day when all will be made right.
When the guilty are forced to face the fullness of their sin with no escape. And those wronged will be restored fully.

May that day come soon. Come quickly Lord Jesus.
