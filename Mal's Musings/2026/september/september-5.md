## Musing 198

"Whoever is not with me is against me, and whoever does not gather with me scatters." (Matthew 12:30)

" for whoever is not against us is for us." (Mark 9:40, Luke 9:50)


Two similar sentences, but in vastly different contexts that give us boundaries on knowing who to partner with. 

In the reference from Matthew, the Pharisees are calling Jesus an ally of Satan, and so Jesus make a clear line in the sand -- either 
you are on His side, or you are actively opposing Him. 

In the Mark and Lukan passages, the disciples encounter a man who was actively working in Jesus' name, but for whatever reason
was not directly following Jesus or the disciples. Who this unknown follower was is known only to God at this point, and Jesus
reminds his disciples that even though this man was not side by side with them, he was still on the same side as Him.

And so, we have a boundary set for us on how to view those who may not be walking beside us, and ultimately, such an evaluation
is centered on Jesus Himself. For the Pharisees, when you reject Jesus, it doesn't matter what else you may agree on (remember, for 
the disciples and the Jewish hearers, the idea that the Pharisees are scatters of God's work and not gatherers would've been 
a massive shock), you are an enemy of the faith. Conversely, if you are truly working in Jesus' name, and not just borrowing
His authority with no real relationship (compare Acts 19:15), it doesn't matter if they are following the same path you are. 

Such is a massive strike against the denominationalism that we so often cling to. While we must be careful of those who 
name their idol Jesus, we should also strive to welcome all worshippers of Jesus under the same banner.
